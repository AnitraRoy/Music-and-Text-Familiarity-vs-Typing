# Typing Performance Under Music and Text Familiarity

## Overview
This project investigates whether **music** (specifically slow-paced or fast-paced music) and **text familiarity** influence typing **speed** and **accuracy**.

## Purpose
The goal of this experiment is to determine if music pace and text familiarity improve or hinder typing efficiency. By comparing performance across familiar and unfamiliar texts under different music conditions, the study evaluates the validity of the common belief that music enhances focus during typing.

## Experimental Design
The experiment was conducted using a sample of enrolled university students.

### **Independent Variables**
1. **Distraction Type (Music Condition)**  
   - **No music**  
   - **Slow-paced music** – *Goodbye in Her Eyes* (81 BPM)  
   - **Fast-paced music** – *Birthday* by The Beatles (140 BPM)  
   Music tempo categories were selected using the Dave Tompkins Music Database.

2. **Text Familiarity**  
   - **Familiar text:** 1000-character excerpts from students' course materials
   - **Unfamiliar text:** 1000-character randomly generated word sets created via MonkeyType to ensure equal unfamiliarity among participants

### **Dependent Variables**
- **Typing speed**, measured in **words per minute (WPM)**  
- **Typing accuracy**, measured as the **percentage of correctly typed characters**

MonkeyType was used to record both WPM and accuracy. Baseline typing skill (no music, unfamiliar text) was also collected for each participant to account for individual differences.

## Results
The experiment found **no statistically significant effects** of music pace or text familiarity on typing speed or accuracy.

**Key findings:**
- Participants typed slightly faster and more accurately with **familiar texts** and **slow-paced music**, though the effect was minimal.  
- **Fast-paced music** created more variability, especially with unfamiliar texts, but not at a significant level.  
- Two-way ANOVA and linear models showed **all p-values > 0.05**, and low adjusted R² values indicated weak predictive power.  
- The **null hypothesis was not rejected**: music pace and text familiarity had **negligible impact** on typing performance.  
- Additional influences—such as **text difficulty, uncommon words, special characters,** and participants adjusting to unfamiliar keyboards—likely played a larger role.

Overall, the results do **not** support the claim that music or text familiarity meaningfully affects typing outcomes, highlighting the need for further research.
